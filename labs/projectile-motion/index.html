Theoretical Mechanics & Derivations

Concept & Formula Breakdown

In classical Newtonian mechanics, two-dimensional projectile motion without air resistance represents the superposition of two independent, mutually perpendicular motions:

  1. Horizontal Axis ($x$): Uniform linear motion with constant velocity $v_x = v_0 \cos\theta$.
  2. Vertical Axis ($y$): Uniformly accelerated motion under gravitational field $a_y = -g$.

1. Trajectory Equation (Parabola)

Eliminating parameter $t$ between $x(t) = (v_0\cos\theta)t$ and $y(t) = (v_0\sin\theta)t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2$ yields the canonical trajectory equation:

$$y(x) = x \tan\theta - \frac{g}{2 v_0^2 \cos^2\theta} x^2$$

Expressed in terms of the total horizontal range $R$:

$$y(x) = x \tan\theta \left( 1 - \frac{x}{R} \right)$$

2. Critical Kinematic Bounds

Time of Flight
$$T = \frac{2v_0 \sin\theta}{g}$$
Maximum Height
$$H = \frac{v_0^2 \sin^2\theta}{2g}$$
Horizontal Range
$$R = \frac{v_0^2 \sin 2\theta}{g}$$

Key Problem-Solving Insights for JEE Advanced

  • Complementary Angle Invariance: Launch angles $\theta$ and $90^\circ - \theta$ produce the exact same horizontal range $R$ for identical launch speed $v_0$.
  • Orthogonality Condition: The velocity vector is perpendicular to the initial velocity vector at time $t = \frac{v_0}{g \sin\theta}$ (achievable only when $\theta > 45^\circ$).
  • Curvature at Apex: At the highest point, velocity is purely horizontal ($v = v_0\cos\theta$), and acceleration is purely normal ($a_n = g$). Hence, the radius of curvature is $\rho = \frac{v_0^2 \cos^2\theta}{g}$.
